01 · Definition

ICT you can actually drive

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) covers the tools we use to locate, save, send, and edit information — phones, computers, software, and the internet that connects them.

Empowerment technology is the slice of ICT built to hand that power to the end user directly. A bakery owner who builds her own online shop with a free website tool, a student who edits video for a class project, a grandmother learning to video-call her family — none of them need a professional to do it for them anymore. That shift, from "hire an expert" to "do it yourself," is the whole idea.

It's not just about access, either. Real empowerment means being information literate — able to judge what's true, cite it fairly, and use it responsibly — and technology literate, meaning comfortable enough with the tools to actually get things done.

02 · A Short History

From read-only pages to a web that talks back

~1991 – 2004

The read-only web

Pages were built by webmasters and simply displayed to visitors. There was no commenting, no liking, no editing — you read what you were given.

  • Static HTML pages, rarely updated
  • One-way: publisher to reader
  • No user accounts or personalization
~2004 – present

The read-write web

The term was popularized around 2004. Suddenly anyone could publish: blogs, wikis, and social platforms turned readers into contributors.

  • User-generated content: posts, comments, reviews
  • Social networking and sharing at scale
  • Mobile apps make publishing instant
emerging now

The intelligent, personalized web

Machine learning and semantic data start tailoring the web to the individual — search that understands intent, assistants that act on your behalf, content that adapts to context.

  • AI-assisted search, writing, and decision-making
  • Personalization based on context, not just clicks
  • Machines interpreting meaning, not just keywords

06 · Stay Safe

Empowerment cuts both ways

The same tools that let you build a business or reach an audience can also expose you. Netiquette and basic online-safety habits are what keep the trade favorable.

Netiquette self-check

0 / 6

Common online threats

Phishing

Messages engineered to look legitimate so you hand over passwords, OTPs, or banking details.

Oversharing

Posting your schedule, location, or full birthdate builds a profile strangers can exploit.

Misinformation

Content designed to spread faster than it can be fact-checked. Verify before you share.

Cyberbullying

Harassment that follows a person from platform to platform. Document it and report it.
